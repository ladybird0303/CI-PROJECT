Key Architectural & Safety Notes

⚡ True Dual-Buck Galvanic Isolation for Relays

The 4-Channel Relay board's jumper shunt is uncapped (removed). Buck-2 directly powers JD-VCC and high-inductive coil switching transients, while Arduino Uno 5V exclusively powers the input optocoupler LEDs (Relay Front VCC). This prevents inductive back-EMF spikes from resetting the MCU or corrupting ESP8266 serial communication.

📊 Whole-Load Inline Current Monitoring

Buck-2 OUT (+) flows into ACS712 Terminal 1 and exits from Terminal 2 directly into the daisy-chained COM terminals of CH1, CH2, and CH3. This captures the aggregated real-time milliamp/ampere draw across the Traffic Light LED, Cooling Fan, and 130 DC Motor.

📶 ESP-01 & LCD Logic Power Stability

ESP8266 Wi-Fi modules require up to 250mA-300mA during Wi-Fi transmission bursts, exceeding the Uno's onboard 5V/3.3V linear regulator limits. Buck-1 provides an independent 5V regulated +ve Rail, backed by a 100nF high-frequency decoupling capacitor across the rails.

🔌 Pinout Summary Checklist

  • Relay Jumper Header (Side): JD-VCC → Buck-2 OUT (+), VCC → Unconnected
  • Relay Signal Header (Front): VCC → Uno 5V, GND → Buck-2 OUT (-), IN1=D7, IN2=D9, IN3=D12
  • Current Sensor (ACS712): Term 1 → Buck-2 OUT (+), Term 2 → Relay COMs, OUT → Uno A3
  • LCD (16x2 I2C 0x27): SDA → Uno A4, SCL → Uno A5, VCC → +ve rail
  • ESP8266 (ESP-01): TX → Uno D10, RX → Uno D11, VCC → +ve rail
  • Buzzer: Buzzer (+) → Uno D8, Buzzer (-) → GND Rail
